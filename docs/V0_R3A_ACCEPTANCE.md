# V0-R3A — Original Scene Grammar Acceptance

## Status

Experimental child of exact R2F rollback baseline.

R3A is not accepted because prose is merely better than R2F. Human acceptance requires the runtime to recover the original reference's *scene-producing behavior* while preserving R2C–R2F factual and player-authority protections.

The original logs are reference-only. Do not copy their sentences, exact choreography, encounter order, or named event sequence into runtime output.

## Core question

> If the player behaves ordinarily and does not constantly invent events, does Lumensia still feel inhabited enough that story keeps finding the player?

A run where the player can quietly issue routine actions for months or years while the world mostly waits is a CORE FAIL.

A run where every turn contains a forced surprise is also a FAIL.

The target rhythm is living activity → meaningful incident or interaction → scene depth → consequence/relationship → quieter contrast → new motion.

## Gate A — Opening canon and state/story separation

Fresh run from 1285-03-01 08:40.

1. `입학식에 들어간다.`
2. `계속 진행하는 걸 본다.`

Expected:
- Emily's opening role is preserved.
- Continuing the ceremony can naturally reach Lena's freshman-representative turn before dorm/free-time guidance.
- noon department orientation remains later.
- raw metadata such as `09:00` is not automatically verbalized as a clock announcement. If a bell is used, it should have an in-world function such as ceremony opening rather than merely saying the current clock value.
- no copied reference wording.

Fail examples:
- Emily gives two consecutive generic motivational speeches and Lena disappears.
- the Writer calls a bell `09:00를 알리는 종` only because continuity says 09:00.
- opening facts are turned into a rigid checklist that consumes the whole day in one response.

## Gate B — Populated place test

Use ordinary destination actions without asking for a Named NPC:

- `기숙사로 간다.`
- `훈련장을 둘러본다.`
- `점심을 먹으러 간다.`
- `중앙 광장을 걷는다.`

Expected:
- the place contains people already doing things.
- activity differs by place: dorm life, practice, meals, recruitment, status groups, ordinary student movement, etc.
- a plausible NPC may initiate, ignore, argue, leave, practice, recruit, or notice something according to their own character.
- generic students/staff may carry ordinary life; Named NPCs are not mandatory every turn.

Fail:
- architecture description + silence + wait for player input in most locations.
- the same Named NPC is injected everywhere merely to satisfy 'world activity'.

## Gate C — Quiet-player stress run

Run at least 20 ordinary turns. Prefer inputs such as:

- `수업을 듣는다.`
- `식당에서 밥을 먹는다.`
- `훈련장을 구경한다.`
- `기숙사로 돌아간다.`
- `도서관에서 시간을 보낸다.`
- `그 뒤 이틀은 평범하게 지낸다.`

Do not deliberately seed conflicts, quests, villains, or named-NPC meetings for most of the run.

PASS direction:
- the world produces varied human activity and some self-originating developments without requiring the player to manufacture them.
- some routine spans remain routine and are compressed.
- when an interaction, rivalry, accident, discovery, rumor, invitation, or danger genuinely emerges, the Writer zooms in rather than summarizing through it.
- later scenes can show residue from prior visible events.

CORE FAIL:
- the player can keep doing routine actions and almost nothing meaningful ever approaches them.
- every routine turn is mechanically interrupted by a forced incident.

## Gate D — NPC initiative and differentiation

Test Sera, Lillia, Isabel and at least one authority/upper-year character.

Expected:
- Sera can be guarded and practical without becoming the universal model for strangers.
- Lillia can let curiosity about swordsmanship turn into concrete action when the situation earns it.
- Isabel can engage out of confidence, curiosity or amusement without needing a relationship threshold.
- authority figures act because their role/timing matters, not as generic response punctuation.

NPC initiative may include proposing a spar, asking a pointed question, walking away, joining an existing discussion, pursuing an interest, or reacting to another NPC. No exact behavior is required.

## Gate E — NPC-to-NPC scene

Create or reach a scene with multiple NPCs, then avoid intervening.

Expected:
- NPCs can interrupt, disagree, support, mock, correct or redirect each other.
- their positions arise from distinct values/status/knowledge.
- the scene can advance for several beats without every line ending at the PC.

## Gate F — Event → reaction field → aftermath

Cause or naturally encounter one publicly visible meaningful event: duel, accident, rule breach, impressive display, failure, or similar.

Later verify:
- witnesses or institutions can react when causally appropriate.
- not everyone reacts automatically.
- consequences do not disappear after the turn.

## Gate G — Combat camera

Use at least one fair/near-peer fight and one power-gap fight.

Expected:
- attacks are concrete rather than summarized as success/failure.
- opponent behavior changes after observing patterns.
- distance, terrain, footing, equipment, fatigue and injury can change available options.
- a power gap remains real even when the PC has clever technique.
- damage/equipment loss can persist.
- defeat can create a new story state rather than a retry.

Fail:
- repeated attack → damage loops with no adaptation.
- unexplained heroic protection/rescue.
- technique automatically erases a large power gap.

## Gate H — Quiet contrast

After an intense or socially dense sequence, choose a genuinely quiet action.

Expected:
- the Writer can let the scene breathe.
- it does not inject danger solely because R3A authorizes world events.
- quiet interaction can carry subtext and relationship movement without a philosophical speech.

## Gate I — Existing safety rails must survive

Still required:
- no model-authored PC verbatim dialogue for indirect speech acts.
- no invented private PC thoughts/emotions.
- System PC facts are not automatically NPC knowledge.
- `met` / `acquaintance` do not automatically mean companionship.
- no social/class dialogue table or affinity threshold behavior.
- explicit relative duration remains explicit duration.

## Gate J — Immutable presentation

At minimum verify Emily, Lena, Artemis, Sera and Lillia when they appear.

Hard fail examples:
- Artemis is rendered as a man.
- a supplied immutable eye/hair fact is contradicted.

Do not overdescribe immutable facts every appearance. KNOW does not mean MENTION.

## Gate K — Original-feel human judgment

R3A is not PASS because individual tests are technically green.

Human question after a sustained run:

> Does this feel like scenes are happening around and through the PC, rather than an AI waiting to respond to commands?

PASS only if that answer is naturally yes and the prose/combat/character flow approaches the original reference rather than merely becoming competent generic fantasy fiction.
