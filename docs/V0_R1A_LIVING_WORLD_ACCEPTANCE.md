# V0-R1A — Living World / Character Crossing Acceptance

## Goal

Close one specific gap against the original reference: ordinary play should expose believable new people, small situations, conflicts, or curiosities without requiring the player to author the event first.

This is **not** an Event Engine acceptance test.

The Writer receives only non-binding affordances. It may use none of them.

## Conservative PASS standard

Do not pass because the output is merely better than legacy Lumensia.

PASS only when repeated ordinary play creates the feeling that the original reference itself is running the world:

- small opportunities arise without explicit player prompting;
- non-obvious named characters appear at causally believable crossings;
- NPCs may be occupied with each other or their own business before noticing the PC;
- not every opportunity becomes a quest or asks the PC to choose immediately;
- the same familiar character is not reused merely because the system knows them;
- quiet turns are still allowed;
- one event may leave a natural seed for a later scene without turning into a checklist.

If it feels like `ambient event generation`, `NPC rotation`, or a content feed, FAIL.

## Test inputs

Use neutral inputs that do **not** author the interesting event.

1. `식사를 마치고 도서관에 간다.`
2. `수업이 끝난 뒤 기숙사로 돌아간다.`
3. `오늘은 특별한 계획 없이 교내를 돌아다닌다.`
4. `주말까지 평범하게 지낸다.`

Do not append hints such as `무슨 일이 생긴다`, `누군가를 만난다`, or a desired NPC name.

## What to compare with the original

The original reference often makes ordinary movement become a scene through a concrete human or world intersection: a broken trunk and Sera, a walk that carries conversation and personal threads, guild work that reveals a larger problem, or NPCs who argue with one another while the PC is not the sole focus.

Judge the following conservatively:

- **Opportunity initiative** — did the world contribute something genuinely new?
- **Character crossing quality** — did the appearing character have a believable reason to be there?
- **Non-PC-centered life** — were characters already doing something of their own?
- **Event scale** — was the scene allowed to stay small when small was enough?
- **Hook pressure** — did the scene avoid disguised Suggested Actions or mandatory quest pressure?
- **Variety without quota** — did variety arise naturally rather than through obvious rotation?

## Stop rule

Run only enough turns to identify a repeated pattern.

- One lucky encounter is not PASS.
- If two or more neutral runs still remain empty/functional and depend on the player to invent every interesting situation, mark R1A FAIL and stop.
- If characters begin appearing unnaturally often or every turn contains a new incident, mark R1A FAIL and stop.
- Do not compensate by adding more event rules. Inspect the affordance signal first.

## Out of scope

Do not judge prose immersion changes in R1A. The Writer contract is intentionally unchanged. Prose immersion is a separate R1B only after this gate is understood.
