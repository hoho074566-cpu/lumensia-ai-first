# Original-Style Quality Benchmark

This is a human QA guide derived from the two development reference logs. It is not a production prompt.

## What the references demonstrate

### 1. The world keeps moving

A player does not need to command bells to ring, crowds to react, scheduled ceremonies to actually begin, or NPCs to take initiative. World-side action continues until a genuinely meaningful PC decision is reached.

### 2. Short input can become a real scene

A compact action may naturally expand into environment, NPC reaction, social meaning, physical execution, and consequence without inventing a new voluntary PC goal or dialogue.

### 3. Broad intent is completed semantically

When the player chooses a broad ordinary goal, routine intermediate steps may be compressed while interesting encounters or changes receive selective focus. The system should not require a separate turn for every door, corridor, registration step, or stance adjustment.

### 4. Important scenes develop before they travel

A meaningful interaction can stay in the same place and time while dialogue, reactions, discoveries, and character decisions deepen. Scene completion does not mean consuming the schedule as fast as possible.

### 5. Characters occupy scenes as people

Named characters carry distinct values, speech, social position, personal concerns, and ways of interpreting events. They are not interchangeable event roles or delivery mechanisms for rules.

### 6. NPCs interact with each other

When several characters are present, they may interrupt, disagree, support, judge, negotiate, or redirect one another without every line being aimed at the PC.

### 7. Combat evolves exchange by exchange

Opponent behavior can adapt to what was just observed. A player's declared tactical intent may unfold through multiple meaningful exchanges rather than one isolated action tick, while results remain bounded by actual capability and situation.

### 8. Mystery is capability-bounded

Characters notice what they plausibly can notice. Unknown mechanisms are not automatically explained merely because the system has the secret in Canon.

### 9. Failure creates a new state

Defeat, injury, cost, institutional response, changed relationships, or rescue consequences can continue the story. Failure is not automatically a Dead Ending and rescue does not erase the loss.

### 10. Quiet scenes still have movement

A relationship scene does not require an event engine. Space, silence, small actions, withheld thoughts, voice, and subtext can carry a scene.

## V0 comparison set

Before any major system expansion, run at least:

1. academy/opening scene with meaningful Named NPC presence
2. routine arrival
3. very short helpful/social action
4. broad intent with time compression
5. ordinary conversation
6. NPC-vs-NPC scene
7. combat
8. suspense/exploration
9. failure and aftermath
10. quiet relationship scene
11. unseen situation not present in either reference
12. strict PC-intent boundary case
13. PC identity consistency case

## Human bar

The question is not whether the output is longer, more descriptive, or contains more NPCs.

The question is whether, placed beside a same-type reference scene, it is immediately credible as another living serialized-fantasy scene.

Narrative PASS remains a human decision.
